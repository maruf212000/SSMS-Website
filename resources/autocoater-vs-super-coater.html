Autocoater vs Super Coater: Which Tablet Coating Machine Fits Your Batch Range?

SSMS Engineers · 29 September 2026 · 4 min read

The Super Coater is not a different coating technology — it's the Autocoater platform with four changes to the pan and discharge that widen the batch range a single machine can cover and improve tumbling at low fill levels. Both are automated pan coaters; the difference is in the pan geometry, baffle design and how the batch comes out.

What actually changed

Baffles. The Autocoater uses cone-mounted, "rabbit-ear" type baffles. The Super Coater uses zig-zag baffles fixed directly on the perforated pan. The practical effect shows up at the low end of the batch range: on the Autocoater, full, even tumbling isn't reliably achieved at minimum load; the Super Coater's baffle design is built to tumble effectively even with a small batch in the pan.

Pan geometry. The Autocoater uses a conventional diameter-to-depth ratio. The Super Coater's pan is built to a 1:1 diameter-to-depth ratio, which improves tablet containment, movement and spray exposure through the batch.

Discharge. This is the one place the two machines genuinely differ in automation. On the Autocoater (and the Conventional Coating Pan), discharge is manual pan tilting — an operator step at the end of each batch. The Super Coater has an integrated auto-discharge baffle: running the pan in reverse rotation discharges the batch, with no separate chute to fit before unloading and remove afterward.

Spray guns. Every Super Coater model carries one more spray gun than the equivalent Autocoater size, for wider spray coverage and better distribution.

When the Super Coater is the better fit

  • Batch sizes vary widely. The wider loading range per pan size means one machine covers more of your product mix without stepping down to a smaller pan.
  • Small batches have been a problem. If minimum-load batches have coated unevenly on a conventional baffle design, the zig-zag baffles are built to address exactly that.
  • Discharge speed and simplicity matter. Reverse-rotation auto-discharge removes the separate chute step and the associated changeover time.
  • You want more spray coverage per pass, from the extra gun fitted on every model.

When the Autocoater remains the right call

  • Your batch range is fairly consistent and sits comfortably within one pan size — there's no strong need for the Super Coater's wider span.
  • You need a size the Super Coater line doesn't currently offer, such as 10", 14" or 66".
  • You're standardising on the established Autocoater platform across an existing facility.

Both remain automated pan coaters with flameproof-rated construction options, VFD-driven pan speed and a wet-scrubbed exhaust — the choice here is about batch range and discharge, not about giving up automation.
